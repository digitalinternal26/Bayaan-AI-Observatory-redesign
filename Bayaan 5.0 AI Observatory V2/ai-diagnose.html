Adoption is progressing faster than capability and governance.

73% of enterprises and 86% of individuals report using AI — but only 12.4% of individuals reach advanced AI capability, only 29% of adopting enterprises have AI governance in place, and just 2% of enterprise AI use is directed at R&D or innovation. Adoption, capability, training and governance are distinct measures; this page keeps them separate rather than folding them into one adoption headline.

The average gap widens with age — 6.5pp at ages 15–24, rising to 10.4pp at 45+. Source: SCAD Individual AI Adoption Survey, 2026 (want-minus-have, self-reported).

AI Insight

The widest reported skills gap is in programming and developing AI apps (+26.2pp) — more than double the gap in AI ethics and responsible use (+10.8pp). The only oversupplied skill is basic AI understanding (−27.6pp): foundational awareness now exceeds what employers ask for, so the constraint has shifted to applied, technical skill.

05 · WORKFORCE IMPACT

Productivity is moving; employment mostly isn't — yet

Dedicated AI-occupation headcount, vacancy and hiring-demand statistics are not yet part of the published 2026 survey. Occupational AI demand is currently tracked qualitatively and will be quantified as this measurement programme matures — a gap that mirrors the leading enterprise barrier reported in Section 03, lack of relevant expertise (16.7%).

06 · TRUST & GOVERNANCE

Confidence is high; trust and governance lag

Sentiment, trust, privacy concern and enterprise governance are distinct measures — sentiment reflects general optimism, trust reflects confidence in AI's judgment specifically, and governance reflects whether adopting enterprises have formal oversight in place.

70.1%positive 43.3%trust in decisions

A 26.8pp gap between individuals holding a positive view of AI overall and those who trust AI specifically in decision-making. Privacy leads concerns, cited by 56.0%.

AI Insight

Abu Dhabi residents remain broadly optimistic and supportive of regulation (88.3%) even as trust in AI decision-making specifically stays conditional (43.3%). Read alongside enterprise governance (29%), the pattern is consistent: residents and enterprises alike appear to be asking for oversight, not simply asking to be reassured.
