What the evidence means, and why it matters — organised by domain, from Abu Dhabi's economy to its people and its infrastructure.

Executive Summary — Economic

Enterprise AI adoption in Abu Dhabi is broad (73%) but unequal: large firms adopt at 88% versus 72% for small enterprises, and sector gaps reach 20 points (66% Other Services to 86% Utilities). Current use is operational, not strategic—71% focuses on administration/production/marketing versus 2% on R&D. Most non-adopters (47%) plan adoption within 3–5 years, signaling intent without urgency. The real constraint is governance: only 29% of adopters have formal AI strategy, creating risk as operations scale and AI footprints deepen.

Executive Summary — People & Skills

Individual AI use far outpaces enterprise adoption at 86%, but skews young (93% of 15–24 year-olds vs. 73% of 45+), exposing a digital dividing line. Advanced capability is rare at 12%, while demand for technical skills (programming, data science) exceeds supply by up to 26 points—a structural bottleneck. Higher education is responding: 74% of students use AI daily or weekly. Sentiment shows an optimism-trust gap: 89% bullish on the UAE's AI future, but only 43% trust AI's judgment, with privacy the primary concern. The data does not yet capture AI's impact on occupational mix and workforce demand.

Executive Summary — Infrastructure

AI adoption is already visible in Abu Dhabi's enterprise and population surveys. The infrastructure that enables AI — compute, cloud platforms, physical connectivity and sustainability — requires a distinct set of indicators. The current evidence provides context through external benchmarks and enterprise technology measures, while SCAD's 2027 roadmap establishes the foundation for measuring infrastructure readiness directly.

AI Insight

Adoption tells us where AI is being used. Infrastructure indicators will tell us what enables it to scale.

Enterprise survey results already show substantial AI adoption, but adoption alone does not reveal compute availability, cloud service dependence, connectivity capacity or the resource footprint of AI. Establishing these measures will allow SCAD to move from observing AI use to understanding the foundation that supports it.
