What the data tells us

Six findings, in the order a policymaker should read them — from the adoption headline, down through capability, skills, enterprise use, governance and trust. Scroll to move through the story, or jump ahead using the rail on the left.

01
Adoption & use

AI adoption is now mainstream, not early-stage

73% of enterprises and 86% of individuals have used AI. Adoption is highest among large enterprises (88%) and the youngest age band (93%, ages 15–24) — but even the slowest-moving groups, small enterprises (72%) and residents 45+ (73%), are past the halfway mark.

02
Awareness & knowledge

Advanced capability remains rare

Only 12.4% of individuals reach the advanced knowledge band, and 42.5% report no knowledge of core AI concepts at all. Adoption without depth is the pattern across every age group — including the youngest, most-adopted one.

03
Skills, training & certification

Skills demand is outrunning training

Programming and developing AI apps shows the widest want-minus-have gap, +26.2pp — ahead of data analysis (+18.9pp) and AI ethics (+10.8pp). The only oversupplied skill is basic AI understanding (−27.6pp). Meanwhile 32.9% intend to enrol in certification within six months, but only 2.3% hold one today.

04
AI in work

Enterprise use is operational, not transformative yet

Administration, production and marketing account for 71% of enterprise AI use between them. Only 2% goes to R&D or innovation — and even that is concentrated in Information & Communication (41.1%) and Professional activities (24.6%).

05
Governance

Governance is the adoption story's weak link

38% of adopting enterprises have no AI strategy or policy in place. 33% have internal policies or guidelines; only 29% have a formal AI strategy. For the 47% of non-adopters planning to adopt within 3–5 years, the top barrier is now expertise (16.7%), not interest.

06
Perceptions, sentiment & concerns

Sentiment is positive; trust is conditional

70.1% of individuals hold a positive view of AI overall, but only 43.3% trust it in decision-making — a 26.8pp gap. Privacy leads concerns at 56.0%. Even so, 88.3% support clear AI regulation, and 88.6% are positive about the UAE's AI future specifically.
