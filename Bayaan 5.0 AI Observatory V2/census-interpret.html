Overview
Abu Dhabi Emirate's population reached 4,441,550 in 2025. Non-Emiratis account for 81.6% of residents, while Emiratis represent 18.4%. Population is concentrated in Abu Dhabi Region, which holds 69% of the Emirate's population, followed by Al Ain (23%) and Al Dhafra (8%). The Emirati population is close to gender balance (50.1% male / 49.9% female), while the non-Emirati population is male-skewed (71.2% male).

Overview
Abu Dhabi's employed population reached 2.98 million in 2025, growing 7.7% from 2024. Non-Emiratis account for 88.8% of employment, while the workforce remains strongly male-dominated at 78.2%. White-collar employment is growing faster than blue-collar (+9.2% vs +6.6%), pointing to a gradual shift toward higher-skilled roles.

Key Insights

Abu Dhabi's employed population reached 2.98M in 2025, an increase of 7.7% from 2024.
Non-Emiratis account for 88.8% of the employed population, reflecting Abu Dhabi's reliance on an international workforce.
The employed population is strongly male-dominated (78.2% male), a pattern most pronounced among non-Emiratis (80.9% male vs Emirati 56.8% male).
White-collar employment is growing faster than blue-collar (+9.2% vs +6.6%), a gradual shift toward higher-skilled roles.
Emirati jobseekers are heavily concentrated among 20–24 year-olds (5,715 of 15,855, or 36%), consistent with the group's broader 71% female skew.
Overview
Abu Dhabi Emirate recorded 326,130 buildings and 899,575 units in 2025, with units growing 2.9% from 2024. Residential uses account for the majority of both buildings (62.3%) and units (58.0%), while Abu Dhabi Region holds the largest share of the built environment — 54.2% of buildings and 69.4% of units.

Key Impacts

Residential use dominates — residential units account for 58.0% of all units, and residential buildings for 62.3% of all buildings.
Abu Dhabi Region is the main concentration — it holds 54.2% of buildings and 69.4% of units, a notably larger share of units than of buildings.
Units significantly exceed buildings — 899,575 units are distributed across 326,130 buildings, an average of 2.76 units per building.
Building and unit composition differ by use — buildings are 62.3% residential by use, while units are 58.0% residential by use, two different lenses on the same built environment.
